Messages appear here. Each one is answered from everyone's words, then learned.

Hi there, say anything or ask me anything. Tell me a story, or share a hot take.

Every reply is drawn as a corridor through the space of all possible texts. Its walls show how much room the model had at each word, and its side doors are other people's words.

Tap a word in the corridor to see where it came from. Tap the price under a question, or the fare meter, to see what the prompt cost and bought.

Everything people have said. Each message is stored in memory and trained into its author's adapter.

    In the Library of Babel nobody writes. Every text is already on a shelf, and the only act is finding it. Here, each memory reply credits the person who asked for the words their question pulled out of other people's texts.

    The library fills memory with copies of everything people wrote, each with a few letters swapped at random, credited to no one. It's Borges' Library of Babel near the stream: every text one typo from yours is already shelved.

    Copies only match a context exactly up to their first swapped letter, so a high swap rate leaves them too far away to be retrieved at all. Around 2% they crowd out the authors.

    Ordinary replies never read the library. Switch a reply's view to “library” to ask the same question with the shelves open. Filling re-reads every copy and takes a while on a laptop.

    The corridor. A model can write any of its 262,145 words at every step, so the space of possible replies is as vast as Borges' Library of Babel. What it actually writes is one path through that space. The corridor's walls show how many words held 95% of the odds at each step, on a log scale: one word is a closed wall, every word is the open library (hatched). Side doors are the likeliest words not taken, underlined in the ink of whoever's stored words pointed there.

    The fare meter. Every word has a price in bits: −log₂ of its probability. Each bit halves the odds. In Babel every word costs the same 18 bits. Your prompt is a fee paid up front, and the meter shows whether it paid for itself compared with an empty prompt.

    Memory replies use a kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., 2020). Weights replies use one LoRA adapter per person (Hu et al., 2021) on the attention layers. Both run on a language model.
